So, PJPT or practical junior penetration tester is an entry level certificate by TCM Security. It cost $249 and according to me, it was completely worth it. TCM Security doesn’t only give you exam attempts, 2 in case you don’t know that, but also the PEH course where Heath Adams is the instructor. I passed the exam on 10th september 2026, and I feel this is a good time to write about it. If you think the exam or the report writing (yes, you do have to write a professional level report to pass this exam) is brutal, wait till you spend a week waiting for the result, it was so brutal for me that, when I received any email, I would think “Oh boy, the result is here, I dont know if I will pass it or not”, but just a heads up, they take 5–7 days to give you your result. So unlike me, actually be patient and just hold your horses.

Let’s start from the start, I will uncover my story and give you some tips from the day I purchased the course to the day I got my result.


Buying the course
I will be completely honest here, I had doubts before buying this course and cert. I knew about TCM Security, but one question kept nagging at me: “Is this a right choice? Should I have just purchased eJPT? Am I wasting my money here?”

Now that I have completed it, I am confident that my choice was correct, rather good. Here’s why: Heath teaches you from the ground up. PEH was really well structured and believe me when I say that everything you need for the exam is in the course.

I remember the date when I purchased it, 8th July, it has been 2 months now, and most of my time went in preparation of the exam. The course will feel a bit long, sections like web pentesting and wifi pentesting won’t be in the exam, so if you feel confident enough to skip them, it will shorten the course drastically — though I wouldn’t recommend it, you’ll learn something new even if you already have experience in the field.

Let’s now discuss the PEH course.

PEH Course
The PEH course starts from an introduction about the field of ethical hacking and as the topic ends, it progresses towards topics which are extremely relevant to the exam like scanning & enumeration, exploitation, and ends with active directory and report writing part. Active directory is, I would say the key to passing the exam and the duration of this topic in the course signifies and proves this. Report writing — I can’t stress it enough, report writing is the only thing that takes you from “I hacked these machines” to “I passed PJPT”. The better the report, higher the chances of you passing the exam.

It took me around 1.5 months to complete the course, I would also like to add that some topics were not new to me, since I have been practising on TryHackMe for around a year now. So if it takes more time, don’t stress and just focus on the work. The key here is, you have to believe in yourself, even when there is a part you think you can’t do, you can. I would also like to appreciate the TCM Security support team, they are amazing, they reply as soon as they can, they are a gem.

Note-taking
Now this is something that could start a war, obsidian or notion? Personally I used notion, hang on, don’t get angry. I was a bit more comfortable with notion and Heath also shares many note-taking apps and websites, use whatever feels comfortable. Take in-depth notes and screenshots not only for revision but also because during the exam, you might wanna revisit a section, and if you have clear notes, it would be really helpful.

What I did was, I took proper notes and screenshots, before the exam, around a week before the exam, I took a notebook and made very short notes that included all the tools and topics, it had all the tools, what they do, what result they give, what kind of credentials like clear text password or hashes do I need to run the tools. It was a really good idea, because instead of searching my long notes, I just had to look at a notebook, which had only 6 pages worth of content and had everything relevant to the topic. If you want those notes, you can message me on LinkedIn.

How to practice?
One downside of the course is that Active Directory is taught using Windows 10, but sourcing a Windows 10 VM for practice can be tricky. I couldn’t find a clean solution — there is a cloud-based method taught in the course, though I found it pretty tough. If you find an easier way, go for it.

Write on Medium
Apart from this you can do rooms on TryHackMe, attacktive directory was an amazing resource, so do practice that. There are capstone machines in the course as well, they help a lot in clearing topics and tools. Read the notes, practice commands and install each tool you learn in the course.

Exam day
This was my first certification exam, and I was extremely nervous about the exam, so nervous that I thought of delaying the exam entirely. Pro tip: you will feel that you are underprepared or you can do better if you wait until the day you get married and your child has started his school, won’t recommend. You do have 2 attempts and the best part is that even if you fail and you submit the report to TCM, they give you hints and actually help you analyse why you failed. So unless you genuinely have no preparation, try to give the exam without any fear and just go for it.

Ok, so now you are ready, you have read everything, you have practiced, you just watched season 2 of Mr. Robot, you click on start exam. You get a ROE and scope. Now, here your bestfriend is enumeration, the more you enumerate the better finding you have and the better report you have. Enumerate as much as possible. As I said, everything you need to know is in the course but occasionally you might use AI during the exam or perhaps while making the report, so did I, but the important part is, you need an AI disclosure in your report, it’s a must. You can use any resource you like during the exam so don’t stress a lot, it’s not like OSCP where you can’t even have a phone with you ( here I am not referring to calling your friend who has passed the exam, please don’t do that ).

During the exam, take as many screenshots as possible to keep a track of what you did and for the report, you need to add everything you did to the report. Before the exam, make sure your kali VM is properly working and each tool is setup and working.

Report writing
The most crucial part of the exam is the report — knowing what to include makes all the difference.

Screenshots, steps of what you did, add CVE or name of any exploit, add remediation and its steps as much as possible, add result and meaning of every image, basically think you are giving this report to a junior IT admin who has to understand and work on your findings without any use of AI and search engines. You might end up going in the wrong route during the exam and maybe you could have taken its screenshot, delete them as soon as you realise you did something wrong because messy screenshots hamper clarity and you will need as much clarity as possible on what you did. I also took wrong screenshots but never deleted them, I can’t even tell you how messy it was.

Another thing you might wanna do is create a small map or diagram of what you did on a notebook and it should contain only correct information. During the course Heath gives you a template for report, you can either reuse it or build it from scratch, I preferred building the entire thing from the top. Your report should be meaningful in a way that even a person who is reading your report for the first time understands what you did, it means adding table of content, creating proper template, choosing readable colors for your tables. The list can go long and long, but the crux to it is “report should be as detailed as possible”.

My report was 75 pages long, yours could be a bit shorter or maybe longer, but size doesn’t matter.

Waiting period
As I stated, the waiting period is brutal, every message you get, you think it’s the result, but it’s just another rejection letter from the jobs you filled the forms for. Jokes aside, TCM says the result can be delivered in 5–7 business days depending on the volume of the reports they have. Can you imagine you just gave your first certification exam and you have to wait around a week to know whether you passed or failed, and not joking, but it took the fun out of the result.
